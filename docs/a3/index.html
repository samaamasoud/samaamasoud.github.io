Stories of Ancient Egypt

Pyramids of Giza

Egypt’s Pyramids of Giza were built to endure an eternity, but now remains one of the ancient world’s greatest mysteries. Here’s what archaeologists know.

Djoser's Step Pyramid

2780 BCE

The architect Imhotep constructed the very first pyramid for King Djoser by stacking six mastabas, with each level being smaller than the one below it to create a stepped shape.

Known as the Step Pyramid, this structure is located at Sakkara near Memphis on the west bank of the Nile River. Similar to the pyramids that were built after it, its interior contains a variety of rooms and passages, which includes the burial chamber of the king.

Bent Pyramid of Snefru

2600 BCE

The transition from the Step Pyramid to a true, smooth-sided pyramid took place during the reign of King Snefru. At Medum, a step pyramid was built, then filled in with stone, and covered with a limestone casing. Nearby at Bahshur, construction was begun on a pyramid apparently planned to have smooth sides.

About halfway up, however, the angle of incline decreases from over 51 degrees to about 43 degrees, and the sides rise less steeply, causing it to be known as the Bent Pyramid. The change in angle was probably made during construction to give the building more stability.

The Red Pyramid

2590 BCE

With his time running out, Snefru demanded a flawless tomb. His architects took the hard-learned lessons from their previous failures and played it completely safe.

They used the shallower, tested 43-degree angle right from the foundation, and the strategy worked perfectly. The red limestone blocks rose in flawless symmetry without any internal cracking. When completed, it became the world's first successful, smooth-sided "true" pyramid, proving that the Egyptians had finally mastered the physics of stone.

The Great Pyramid of Giza

The Pyramid of Khufu

2589 - 2566 BCE

The largest and most famous of all the pyramids, the Great Pyramid of Giza, was commissioned by Snefru's son, Khufu. The pyramid's base covered over 13 acres and its sides rose at an angle of 51 degrees 52 minutes and were over 755 feet long. It originally stood over 481 feet high; today it is 450 feet high. Scientists estimate that its stone blocks average over two tons apiece, with the largest weighing as much as fifteen tons each.

Two other major pyramids were built at Giza, for Khufu's son, King Khafre, and a successor of Khafre, Menkaure. Also located at Giza is the Sphinx, a massive statue of a lion with a human head, carved during the time of Khafre.
